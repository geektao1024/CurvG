An AI Manim animation tool is only useful if the mathematics on screen is right. A beautiful video with a wrong derivative is worse than no video. That is why CurvG does not ask a language model to write each animation from scratch. Instead, the AI does one small job, choosing a verified template and filling in its parameters, and code that we wrote and tested does the rest.

**Key takeaway:** In CurvG, an AI picks one of 12 verified templates and fills in its parameters; every number on screen is computed in code before the render; and every video comes with its Manim Community source. Topics outside the templates go to a free-form generator that is still a beta and can get the mathematics wrong.

## What does an AI Manim animation generator actually generate?

It doesn't draw a video from words. [Manim Community](https://docs.manim.community/en/stable/tutorials/quickstart.html) is a Python engine: an animation is a `Scene` whose `construct()` method adds objects and plays animations, and Manim renders the frames. So any "AI Manim" tool ends up producing Python that Manim runs.

There are two ways to get that Python:

1. **The AI writes the whole scene.** Every object, coordinate, label and timing is generated fresh for each request.
2. **The AI chooses a scene that already exists.** A template is a tested scene with parameters (the function, the range, the number of rectangles), and the AI only fills those in.

CurvG uses the second way for every paid video, and keeps the first as a beta.

## Why templates instead of AI-written scenes?

Because a scene written from scratch is a new program every time. Any object, coordinate or timing in it can be wrong, a single wrong number spoils the video, and an automated math check can only catch some of those mistakes.

A template turns that around:

- **The mathematics is computed, not generated.** A template computes the numbers it shows (a derivative, a Riemann sum, a root, a determinant) in TypeScript before the scene is built. Values it cannot compute exactly, such as a point where the function has no derivative or an integral that diverges, are rejected with a reason before you spend anything.
- **Every parameter goes through one check.** The form, the AI's choice and links from elsewhere on the site all pass the same validation, on the server, before every render.
- **The same parameters give the same video.** Timings sit on a fixed grid, and the renderer checks the finished MP4 against the planned length.

## How does a topic become a video in CurvG?

### Step 1: Say what you want to explain

Write it the way you would say it in class: "the tangent to x³ − 3x at x = 1", "a Riemann sum of x² from 0 to 2 with 8 rectangles", "how a 2×2 matrix with determinant −1 flips the plane". You can also start from a [lab chapter](/labs) or a [curve](/curves): many of them open the matching template with the numbers already filled in, without asking the AI at all.

### Step 2: The AI picks a template and fills in the parameters

This is a small, fast request. The result goes through the same parameter check as the form, so a request the template can't film exactly is refused with a reason instead of rendered badly.

### Step 3: Check the parameters

You see the template's form: change the function, the range, the point, the number of rectangles or harmonics, the aspect ratio (16:9 or 9:16) and the theme (light or dark). A sketch in the browser shows what will be drawn before anything renders.

### Step 4: Render

Manim Community renders the MP4 in the cloud. The explanation appears as on-screen captions, in English or Chinese; there is no voice-over. Every account with a verified email gets 3 free preview videos (720p, watermarked); clean 1080p videos use credits ([pricing](/pricing)), and a preview can be exported in HD later. Failed renders refund their credits.

### Step 5: Keep the code

Every video comes with its Manim Community source, which you can read, copy and download. You can't edit it in the browser, but you can run and change it in your own Manim setup.

## What happens when no template fits?

The request goes to the free-form generator, where an AI writes the scene from scratch. It runs an automated math review, but that review warns rather than blocks: if it finds a problem, you see the warning and decide. It can fail, and it can get the mathematics wrong, so watch a free-form video before you show it to a class. Failed renders are refunded here too.

## How do you write a request that maps cleanly to a template?

Name the mathematical object and the numbers, not the style:

- **Good:** "Derivative of sin(x) at x = π/4 as a tangent line." The template and both parameters are clear.
- **Good:** "Fourier series of a square wave with 7 harmonics."
- **Vague:** "A cool calculus animation." The AI has to guess the function, the point and the template.

If your topic is close to a template but not the same, say which part matters most; the AI will choose the nearest template, and you can adjust the parameters before rendering.

## What should you check before you use the video?

- **That you asked for the right thing.** The template computes correctly whatever function and range you give it, so a typo in the function is the most likely error.
- **Readability.** Captions are scaled to fit, but long formulas on a 9:16 frame get small. Try both aspect ratios if the video is for a phone.
- **Free-form warnings.** A beta video with a warning needs a careful watch, line by line.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can AI guarantee that a math animation is correct?

Not on its own. In CurvG's template videos the AI only chooses the template and its parameters; the numbers on screen are computed in code and the parameters are validated, so the mathematics is as reliable as the template. Free-form videos are written by the AI and can be wrong.

### Do I need to know Python?

No. You describe the topic or fill in the template's form. The Manim source is there if you want to read it or take it further.

### Does CurvG always return a video?

For template topics, yes, unless the render itself fails, in which case the credits are refunded. Free-form beta requests can fail.

### Can I edit the Manim code?

Not in the browser. Download it and edit it in your own Manim environment.

### Does CurvG replace Manim Community?

No. Manim Community is the engine that renders every CurvG video. CurvG is an independent product and is not affiliated with the Manim Community or 3Blue1Brown.
